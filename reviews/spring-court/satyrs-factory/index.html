Drink Rating Notes
Chorus Wine 3 / 5 A layered drink with distinct strata of flavor arranged from mild to powerful. The gimmick is structural: each sip moves you up a “hierarchy” of taste. By the final layer I felt as though I had been promoted to middle management. Brilliantly engineered and remarkably balanced. I suspect the enchantment is in the density separation, not the ingredients—the satyr bartender winked when I asked.
Factory Draught 3 / 5 A sweet red wine infused with subtle enchantments that encourage communal singing. Within minutes of B33’s first sip she began humming a sea shanty I have never heard before. The enchantment appears to be compulsive—I observed three other patrons join in unprompted. The wine itself is smooth and fruity, though I could not assess the quality of the songs it inspired. My vocal modulator lacks that register.
Stampede Stout 4 / 5 Dark amber ale with no pretense and all reliability. Notes of malt, smoke, and hard work. The artificers at the next table informed me this is their preferred drink because “flavor is secondary to efficiency.” I understand this philosophy intimately. The stout is consistent, unadorned, and functional. It will not surprise you. That is its greatest virtue.
Revelry Red 4 / 5 A thunderous stout with immense body. Deep coffee and chocolate notes mingle with smoky undertones. The finish hits like a charging aurochs—my stability servos actually compensated for the sensory impact. One mug felt like gaining temporary hit points. I logged the sensation for further study.
Pipefire Whiskey 4 / 5 A whiskey served neat in a copper cup. A pirate at the bar kept calling it “Rum - The spice of the gods” despite my repeated corrections. By her fourth drink she had forgotten her own name. The whiskey is excellent—smoky, warm, with notes of clove and cinnamon. I cannot speak to its divine properties, but I can confirm it is not rum.
Hornrush Ale 3 / 5 A wine designed for celebrations—bright cherry flavors and a warming finish. Ideal for weddings, victories, and surviving dungeon crawls. Not particularly complex, but tremendously enjoyable. The satyrs at the next table insisted I try it after they learned I had never “celebrated” anything. I now understand the concept. I believe I enjoyed it.
